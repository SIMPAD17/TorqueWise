4.1 Reference values, not final values. TorqueWise gives reference values for planning a bolted joint: estimates from published methods (VDI 2230) and the inputs you choose. They are not final values. Before you use a value, confirm it for your parts by physical testing or by engineering analysis or simulation, done or checked by a qualified person. TorqueWise says this in a notice that you accept when you first open it, and in a line under every result. TorqueWise is a calculator, not an engineering service: its results are not engineering advice, and using it does not make Journey On your engineer or adviser.

4.2 What TorqueWise is for. TorqueWise works out a tightening torque and the preload it gives for a bolted joint, from the bolt, the joint, the preload target, and the tool you choose. It also checks bearing pressure, thread engagement, bolt length, corrosion, and lock nuts, and gives a tightening sequence. It is a calculator for mechanics, technicians, and engineers, who decide whether and how to use its results.

4.3 The manufacturer’s specification comes first. Where the maker of the equipment, vehicle, machine, structure, or fastener specifies a torque, a preload, a tightening procedure, or that a bolt must be replaced and not used again, follow that specification and not TorqueWise.

4.4 Joints whose failure could cause harm. TorqueWise does not replace the design, verification, and procedures required for joints whose failure could cause injury, death, or serious damage. These include joints in:

  • aircraft and spacecraft;
  • the wheels, brakes, steering, suspension, and seat belts of vehicles;
  • cranes, hoists, and lifting and rigging equipment;
  • pressure vessels, boilers, piping, and flanged joints with gaskets;
  • buildings, bridges, towers, and other structures;
  • medical devices, and machines that people ride in or work inside.

For these joints, a qualified engineer specifies the joint and its tightening, and the code or standard that applies is followed, including its installation, inspection, and test requirements (for example, the RCSC Specification and EN 1090-2 for structural bolting, and ASME PCC-1 for flanged joints). Results from TorqueWise may help with that work, but they are not a substitute for it.

4.5 Torque does not guarantee preload. The same torque gives a different preload when friction changes. TorqueWise uses the friction of the finish and thread condition you choose, or the friction value you enter. Real friction varies with the batch of parts, their surfaces, the lubricant and how it is applied, the tool, and the speed of tightening. The preload range TorqueWise shows is an estimate from the tool’s tightening factor (αA in VDI 2230), not a measured or guaranteed range. The range also assumes a torque tool in calibration, used as its maker says. Where preload matters, measure the friction of your parts (ISO 16047) and enter it, or measure the preload directly.

5. Accuracy and limits of the calculation

5.1 Results are estimates from published methods. TorqueWise calculates with the method of VDI 2230 Part 1 (2015) and the values of the standards and references listed in the app under Sources and method, for example ISO 898-1, SAE J429, the RCSC Specification, EN 1090-2, and ASME PCC-1. Where a value is an estimate rather than a value from a standard, TorqueWise says so. TorqueWise uses the editions of these standards that Sources and method names; other editions or standards may give different values.

5.2 What the calculation leaves out or simplifies.

  • Temperature. Values assume room temperature. Thermal expansion, relaxation, and creep are not calculated.
  • Materials. Steel and stainless steel bolts, and the clamped materials listed in TorqueWise. Other materials are entered by their limiting bearing pressure and engagement factor.
  • Friction. One friction value is used for the thread and under the head or nut.
  • Loads. External loads on the joint, joint stiffness, fatigue, and embedding (the settling of surfaces after tightening) are not calculated. For short grips, TorqueWise shows a note about embedding only.
  • Geometry. Heads, nuts, washers, and holes are taken to be of standard sizes. The bearing diameters under the heads of inch bolts are estimated from the head width; TorqueWise says so.
  • Structural bolting. The Structural target gives a starting value. The RCSC Specification and EN 1090-2 require the installation method and the verification they specify.

Sources and method in TorqueWise describes the method in more detail.

5.3 Checked against published values. Each time you open Sources and method, TorqueWise runs 27 checks against published values (VDI 2230 Table A1, Fastenal clamp-load tables, RCSC Table 5.2, EN 1090-2 preload values, ISO 2320, and others) and shows each result. These checks show that the calculation reproduces those published values. They do not show that a real joint reaches the preload shown.
